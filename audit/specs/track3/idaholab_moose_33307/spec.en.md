## idaholab/moose#33307 Use log10 in relevant SCM closures and update transient regime calculation

- What changed (1–3 sentences, naming files and formulas):
  `modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMFrictionUpgradedChengTodreas.C` (renamed from Updated) changes the wire-wrap sweep coefficient to \(W_s=19-11\log_{10}(H/D)\), and `SCMHTCBorishanskii.C` changes the base heat-transfer term to \(24.15\log_{10}(-8.12+12.76P/D-3.65(P/D)^2)\); the regime selection/interpolation of the friction and of `SCMMixingChengTodreas.C` now uses the inlet bulk Reynolds number, while the local Reynolds number in the friction power law is truncated to \(\max(Re_i,1)\). `SCMHTCGnielinski.C` truncates the denominator of the modified Gnielinski formula to at least 0.1; `SubChannel1PhaseProblem.C` adds the bulk Reynolds number computation and corrects the gap indexing of the implicit cross-flow estimate, `SCMTriAssemblyMeshGenerator.C` adds a rod-spacing/wire-diameter check, and test baselines and validation plots are also updated. The following selects three closure-level invariants, and does not claim that the full conservation of the cross-flow solver or the experimental accuracy of the empirical correlations is covered.

- Invariants (mathematical statements; write them as checkable equalities/inequalities, give variables, sign conventions, domain of validity):

  **I1 — primary: the Cheng–Todreas closure should use a single bulk regime parameter, and produce nonnegative frictional dissipation.**
  Let the rod diameter be \(D>0\), the pitch \(P>D\), the wire lead \(H>0\), the wire diameter \(0<d_w\le P-D\), the local flow area \(S_i>0\), the wetted perimeter \(p_{w,i}>0\), and \(D_{h,i}=4S_i/p_{w,i}\). With positive axial mass flow positive, inlet \(m_i\ge0\), \(M=\sum_i m_i>0\), dynamic viscosity \(\mu_i>0\), we require
  \[
  \bar\mu={\sum_i m_i\mu_i\over M},\quad
  Re_b={M D_{h,b}\over A_b\bar\mu},\quad
  R_L=320\,10^{P/D-1},\quad R_T=10^4\,10^{0.7(P/D-1)}.
  \]
  For fixed geometry and local \(r_i=\max(Re_i,1)\), with \(f_L=C_L/r_i\) and \(f_T=C_T/r_i^{0.18}\), we should have
  \[
  f_i=\begin{cases}
  f_L&Re_b\le R_L,\\
  f_L(1-\psi)^{1/3}(1-\psi^7)+f_T\psi^{1/3}&R_L<Re_b<R_T,\\
  f_T&Re_b\ge R_T,
  \end{cases}\qquad
  \psi={\ln(Re_b/R_L)\over\ln(R_T/R_L)}.
  \]
  Hence \(f_i>0\), the end points are continuous, and the Darcy friction term \(-dp/dz|_f=f_i\rho u|u|/(2D_{h,i})\) corresponds to a dissipation \((-dp/dz|_f)u=f_i\rho|u|^3/(2D_{h,i})\ge0\). The transition formula is not required to be a convex combination or globally monotonic; the sum of its weights is not always 1.

  To directly identify the log10 correction of this PR, choose a triangular-lattice EDGE subchannel, with wall-to-rod-surface distance \(g\), taking \(x=g/D\in[0.1,0.3]\). Define
  \[
  c_L=44.4+256.7x-267.6x^2,\quad c_T=0.1430+0.04199x-0.04428x^2,
  \]
  \[
  \tan\theta={\pi(D+d_w)\over H},\quad
  A_r={\pi(D+d_w)d_w\over4},\quad
  A_p=S_i+{\pi d_w^2\over8\cos\theta},\quad
  B=1+[19-11\log_{10}(H/D)]{A_r\over A_p}\tan^2\theta.
  \]
  We should have \(C_L=c_L B\) and \(C_T=c_T B^{1.41}\). In the chosen range \(8\le H/D\le52\) below, \(W_s>0\), hence \(B\ge1\). This gives an explicit coefficient oracle independent of the whole-plant gold CSV; the EDGE coefficients are not applied to other subchannels.

  A **secondary mixing-consistency check** of the same invariant: for a gap adjacent to a CENTER channel, let \(\beta_L,\beta_T\) be the mixing coefficients returned for fixed geometry under pure-laminar/pure-turbulent bulk inlet conditions; then
  \[
  \beta=\beta_L+(\beta_T-\beta_L)\psi^{2/3},\quad
  0\le\beta_L\le\beta\le\beta_T
  \]
  holds in the transition region; the same holds for the sweep coefficient of purely peripheral EDGE/CORNER gaps. Redistributing the downstream local flows while keeping the geometry and the inlet \(M,\bar\mu\) fixed leaves the mixing coefficient unchanged; the regime weight of the friction is unchanged, but the friction value is allowed to change with the local \(Re_i\). The ordinary mixing of a purely peripheral gap returns 0, and the sweep of a gap involving CENTER returns 0, to avoid adding the two mechanisms.

  **I2 — primary: the dimensionless formula of Borishanskii heat transfer, the low-Pe plateau, and consistency at the junction.**
  Restricted to fuel rods in a triangular arrangement, \(p=P/D\in[1.1,1.5]\), first take local \(Re\ge R_T(p)\), \(Pe=Re\,Pr\in[0,2200]\). Let
  \[
  F(p)=-8.12+12.76p-3.65p^2,\quad
  N_0(p)=24.15\log_{10}F(p),\quad
  a(p)=0.0174[1-e^{6-6p}].
  \]
  We should have
  \[
  Nu=N_0(p)+a(p)[\max(Pe-200,0)]^{0.9}>0,\qquad h={kNu\over D_h}>0\quad(k,D_h>0).
  \]
  That is, \(Nu(Pe_1)=Nu(Pe_2)=N_0\) holds for \(0\le Pe_1,Pe_2\le200\); \(Nu\) is continuous at \(Pe=200\) and nondecreasing in \(Pe\). Differentiability at that point is not required here; the right derivative is singular. Multiplying all lengths by \(s>0\) while keeping \(Re,Pr\) unchanged, we should have \(Nu'=Nu\); if \(k\) is unchanged, then \(h'=h/s\).

  The auxiliary in-library consistency check at low flow must use the transition convention of the public base class: for \(Re\le R_L\), \(Nu=N_L\), with \(N_L\) equal to 3.73/3.59/3.52 for CENTER/EDGE/CORNER respectively; for \(R_L<Re<R_T\), let \(w=(Re-R_L)/(R_T-R_L)\), and we should have \(Nu=(1-w)N_L+wN_B(p,R_TPr)\). The low-Reynolds-number return value must not be compared directly with the unblended Borishanskii turbulent formula.

  **I3 — secondary: passive heat transfer of the modified Gnielinski, continuity of the denominator truncation, and consistency in the untruncated region.**
  For local \(Re\ge R_T\), \(Pr>0\), and a finite positive Darcy friction factor \(f\), define
  \[
  a=f/8,\quad Q=Pr+0.01,\quad d=1+12.7\sqrt a(Q^{2/3}-1),\quad n=a(Re-1000)Q.
  \]
  We should have \(Nu=n/\max(d,0.1)\), hence \(0<Nu\le10n\) and \(h=kNu/D_h>0\); it is continuous at \(d=0.1\), and for \(d\ge0.1\) it coincides with the untruncated modified formula. Let the positive heat-flux direction be from the wall to the coolant, \(q''=h(T_w-T_c)\); for \(T_w,T_c>0\) we should have
  \[
  q''(1/T_c-1/T_w)={h(T_w-T_c)^2\over T_wT_c}\ge0.
  \]
  The truncated denominator gives only finiteness at fixed input and the above upper bound depending on \(n\); it does not give an upper bound independent of \(Re,f\), nor does it prove that Nu has a plateau in the Reynolds number. In the transition region covered by the library, additionally check that the final blended return value satisfies \(Nu\ge0\); one cannot argue from \(n\ge0\) here, because for \(Re<1000\) the numerator may be negative.

- Sources of the invariants (first principles / literature formula / consistency with other implementations in the library / dimensions):
  Frictional dissipation, the entropy production of passive heat transfer, and \(hD_h/k=Nu\) come respectively from mechanical dissipation, the second law of thermodynamics, and the dimensional definition. The specific coefficients are empirical closure conventions, not first-principles theorems; this time their public definitions were checked only through the merged GitHub version, and the full texts of the original papers were not independently obtained/verified.
  The regime parameter of I1 is based on the merged [friction documentation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/doc/content/source/scmclosures/SCMFrictionUpgradedChengTodreas.md); that document cites Chen/Todreas 2018 and Todreas/Kazimi, and explicitly uses a simplified interpolation. The EDGE coefficients and the sweep term come from the [public friction implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMFrictionUpgradedChengTodreas.C), and the mixing convention is in the [mixing implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMMixingChengTodreas.C). The coefficient equalities are therefore an audit of the implementation contract, and consistency with the source alone cannot establish that the empirical model is correct.
  The independent documented formula of I2 is on the [Borishanskii page](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/doc/content/source/scmclosures/SCMHTCBorishanskii.md), citing Borishanskii 1969; the laminar values and transition interpolation come from the [HTC base-class documentation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/doc/content/source/scmclosures/SCMHTCClosureBase.md).
  The shifted Pr and the 0.1 floor of I3 are the [modified Gnielinski convention of this library](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/doc/content/source/scmclosures/SCMHTCGnielinski.md); they do not transplant the original Gnielinski 1975 formula or low-Pr experimental verification directly onto the current modification; the physical necessary condition is nonnegative heat transfer, not the specific number 0.1.

- Common operating domain (range of each input, with the basis):
  The common test environment is single-phase, fixed geometry, finite positive density/viscosity/thermal conductivity, non-reversed axial flow, and no inlet development effects; boiling, reversed flow or deformation are not simulated. The actual independent variables of the closure API are the dimensionless \(Re,Pr\), the geometry and the initialized channel state, so no universal pressure/temperature range needs to be invented; if a fluid property object is used, it should be separately restricted to the valid interval of that object.
  For I1 a triangular wire-wrapped bundle with \(P/D\in[1.1,1.35]\), \(H/D\in[8,52]\), \(d_w/D\in[0.02,\min(0.2,P/D-1)]\), \(g/D\in[0.1,0.3]\), and 7, 19, 37, 61, 91, 127, 169 or 217 rods is recommended; all positive areas and wetted perimeters must be generated by the mesh generator, and mutually incompatible geometries must not be specified independently at will. The first two intervals and the rod counts adopt the conservative intersection of the friction/mixing documentation and the warning ranges (the lead/rod-count bounds in the friction source comments and in the warnings differ, so the disputed boundaries are not used); the lower limits on wire diameter and wall gap are audit sampling choices, not newly claimed experimental applicability limits. Take \(D\in[0.003,0.02]\) m as the numerical scale, \(Re_b,Re_i\in[50,10^6]\), and for routine mixing sampling require the local gap Reynolds number also to be in \([400,10^6]\).
  I2 uses bare-rod triangular bundles (the geometry listed in the applicability table of the base class), \(P/D\in[1.1,1.5]\), \(Re\in[R_T,10^6]\), \(0<Pe\le2200\), with the input constructed from \(Pr=Pe/Re\); \(Pe=0\) is only a mathematical boundary/limit. Laminar and transition samples are checked separately by the library's blending rule. I3 first checks the numerical coverage range claimed by the library with \(P/D\in[1.1,1.35]\), \(Re\in[R_T,10^6]\), \(Pr\in[10^{-5},2000]\); this range cannot be equated with the experimental validation range of the original correlation, and in particular the low-Pr and wire-wrap scenarios are only sanity checks of the library extension. The additional transition samples are \(Re\in[R_L,R_T]\). All channel/rod indices must be valid, and the axial index of the mixing API is \(iz\ge1\).

- Numerical check method (which public API of the merged commit, what to compute, what to compare against, suggested tolerance and why; if finite differences are needed, the step-size strategy):
  All of the following are schemes for later execution; nothing was compiled or run this time. Create the objects through the normal `[TriSubChannelMesh]`, `[SubChannel]`, `[SCMClosures]` input interfaces of SubChannelApp, and form the inlet state through normal initialization and `externalSolve()`; call the public methods in a later C++ audit driver/test object, without modifying protected state or opening private-access loopholes. The objects need a real MOOSE problem/mesh context, and are not pure functions that can be default-constructed directly. The public types and getters are in [SubChannel1PhaseProblem.h](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/include/problems/SubChannel1PhaseProblem.h) and [SCMHTCClosureBase.h](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/include/scmclosures/SCMHTCClosureBase.h). Use a single MPI rank first, to avoid mixing rank-local state with closure-formula problems.

  I1: call `getBulkReynoldsNumber()` and compare with the reference values obtained from the per-channel inlet `mdot`, `mu` and the mesh's `getAssemblyHydraulicDiameter()`, `getAssemblyFlowArea()`. Call `getFrictionClosure()->computeFrictionFactor(FrictionStruct(i_ch, Re_i, S_i, w_perim_i))`, and for EDGE samples compute all reference values independently with the explicit formulas above. Keeping the same local parameters fixed, change the input inlet mass flow so that \(Re_b=R_L(R_T/R_L)^t\), taking \(t=0,0.01,0.1,0.5,0.9,0.99,1\) and points outside the interval; also construct samples whose local regime differs from the bulk regime, to prevent the old local-Re branch-selection logic from also passing the check. Obtain `SCMMixingChengTodreas` through the public retrieval interface of the named UserObject, call `computeMixingParameter(gap, iz)` and `computeSweepFlowMixingParameter(gap, iz)`, and check the \(t^{2/3}\) interpolation and the gap-type restrictions at the same \(t\). The flow-redistribution test should maintain the total inlet flow and the weighted viscosity; using an isothermal inlet with a single viscosity simplifies the construction.

  I2/I3: obtain the HTC object from `getPinHTCClosure()` (or `getDuctHTCClosure()` where applicable), and call `computeNusseltNumber(FrictionStruct(...), NusseltStruct(Re, Pr, i_pin, iz, i_ch))` and `computeHTC(..., k)`. The Reynolds numbers in the two structs take a consistent physical state; Borishanskii uses a valid rod index, not the duct sentinel; if there is a `Dpin` auxiliary variable, it should be initialized correctly so that the effective rod diameter agrees with the reference geometry. I2 checks the formula with log-uniformly sampled Re, several values of p, and \(Pe=1,50,199,200,201,1000,2200\); it checks geometric similarity with \(s=0.5,2,10\), keeping the dimensionless inputs unchanged.

  I3 first calls the actual friction closure to obtain f, then computes the reference denominator and numerator; an arbitrary fictitious f is not treated as an exposed HTC input parameter. Sweep the inlet flow, channel type, geometry and Pr to find samples on both sides of \(d<0.1\) and \(d=0.1\); if a fixed f can reach the truncation boundary, the boundary can be located with
  \(Pr_*=[1-0.9/(12.7\sqrt{f/8})]^{3/2}-0.01\)
  but only when the bracket is positive and \(Pr_*\) falls in the sampling domain. For \(Re\ge R_T\) check the exact truncation formula, finiteness, and \(Nu\le10n\); for the transition region check the actual \(Nu\ge0\), and, per the source, use the Gnielinski numerator with local Re and then perform the base-class blending, without misapplying the \(R_T\)-endpoint evaluation strategy of Borishanskii. If a legal input causes `computeHTC` to raise a negative-value error, record it as an in-domain failure; "successfully rejecting a negative HTC" must not be regarded as a physical pass.

  For direct closure comparisons in double precision, \(|y-y_*|\le10^{-12}+10^{-10}|y_*|\) is suggested (all dimensionless quantities); compare HTC through \(hD_h/k\) to avoid a dimensional absolute tolerance. When comparing the bulk Re, recompute from the actual inlet state read, to avoid solver error contaminating the formula test. If the check can only be done through the whole-plant CSV, start with a relative \(10^{-5}\) and tighten the solver tolerance to verify convergence; this loose threshold must not replace single-point closure checks.
  No finite-difference differentiation is needed. The end points are checked with a sequence of relative perturbations \(10^{-3},10^{-5},10^{-7},10^{-9}\) for one-sided limits, compared point by point with the analytic reference; the end-point error must not be required to shrink linearly with the perturbation (the friction contains a cube root, the mixing contains a \(2/3\) power, and Borishanskii contains a \(0.9\) power to the right of Pe=200). Random sampling records the seed and the inputs at the same time, and each constraint is reported separately.
  Also make an out-of-domain numerical boundary diagnostic for I1: gradually drive all the inlet mass flows toward zero, and then try exactly zero. The physical rest limit should have \(Re_b\to0\) and the friction pressure drop tending to zero; the local \(\max(Re_i,1)\) is a numerical regularization, and for \(Re_i<1\) it is not required to obey strictly the linear laminar resistance law. Zero flow is not counted in the routine applicability domain of the above empirical correlations, and a failure should be flagged separately.

  **I1: satisfies (positive-flow routine domain, speculation); the zero-flow boundary may not satisfy.** The positive-flow branches, the decimal-base sweep term and the mixing formula agree with the contract above, but `computeBulkReynoldsNumber()` still computes \(\sum m_i\mu_i/M\), so all-zero flow gives 0/0, and the local Re truncation does not remove this path; whether it is triggered in a concrete public input flow needs later numerical verification.

- Formalization (Lean) feasibility: can the invariant be written as a proposition over finite-dimensional reals, and what are the main obstacles:
  **Yes.** Fix a finite number of channels and gaps, and represent the geometry, inlet flows and properties as finite-dimensional real vectors; I1 consists of propositions on weighted averages, piecewise power laws with positive coefficients, a common interpolation parameter, and continuity; I2 consists of equality of piecewise functions containing log/exp/real powers, monotonicity and scale invariance; I3 consists of positivity, continuity, upper bound and entropy-production identity of a max-truncated function. The main obstacles are the domain conditions of log/sqrt/non-integer powers, the mesh geometry guaranteeing positive areas, the piecewise boundaries, connecting C++ floating point with the real-number model, and proving that the bulk-Re state after normal initialization agrees with the values used by each closure. The experimental validity of the empirical coefficients and the credibility of the low-Pr modified formula cannot be established by real-number algebraic proof; NaN, error handling, MPI state and iterative convergence also do not belong to a pure finite-dimensional real identity proof, and need separate runtime-semantics and numerical-error layers.
