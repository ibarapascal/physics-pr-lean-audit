## idaholab/moose#34020 Pass the nodal slip rate to dynamic mortar function_friction

- What changed (1–3 sentences, naming files and formulas):

  `modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C` changes the old-time-step velocity passed to the velocity-dependent friction function from a sum without integration weights to the nodal projected velocity \(\mathbf s_i=\mathbf W_i/D_i\), stores the history only on the process that owns the degree of freedom, and treats degrees of freedom without history as the zero vector; the constant parameter `mu` gains a `mu > 0` validation. `ComputeDynamicWeightedGapLMMechanicalContact.C` accumulates and communicates the denominator \(D_i\) whether or not `normalize_c` is turned on; the corresponding header, object documentation and `mortar_dynamics/gold/frictional-mortar-3d-dynamics-light-function_out.e` are updated in step. The following takes the merged-commit source as authoritative; all numerical checks are schemes yet to be executed, and no computation was run this time.

- Invariants (mathematical statements; write them as checkable equalities/inequalities, give variables, sign conventions, domain of validity):

  **Common notation and boundaries.** In 2D or 3D contact, take \(k=1\) or \(2\) orthonormal nodal tangent vectors \(\mathbf t_{ia}\). Define the relative velocity \(\mathbf v_r=\mathbf v_{secondary}-\mathbf v_{primary}\), the compressive normal multiplier \(p_i>0\), and the tangential multiplier vector \(\boldsymbol\lambda_i\in\mathbb R^k\); in this library the tangential multiplier is in the same direction as the slip, and the physical friction traction acting on the secondary takes the negative sign. Let \(\psi_i\) be the dual mortar test function actually used and \(w_q=J_q\omega_q\,coord_q\) the geometric, quadrature and coordinate factors on the same mortar contact integration domain, and define
  \[
  D_i^n=\sum_q\psi_i(q)w_q^n>0,\qquad
  W_{ia}^n=\sum_q\psi_i(q)w_q^n\,\mathbf v_r^n(q)\cdot\mathbf t_{ia}^n,
  \qquad s_{ia}^n=W_{ia}^n/D_i^n.
  \]
  These assertions are made only for degrees of freedom with non-degenerate contact support that have not been removed by edge dropping. The dual test function may take negative values, so it cannot be required on this basis that the projected velocity always lies between the minimum and maximum of the velocities at all quadrature points.

  **I1 — Primary: constant reproduction, integration scale and partition invariance of the projected velocity.** The nodal history velocity actually used by the friction function must satisfy \(D_i\mathbf s_i=\mathbf W_i\) above. In planar contact with a fixed tangent basis and \(\mathbf v_r(q)\cdot\mathbf t_{ia}=U_a\), we must have \(s_{ia}=U_a\), independent of the number of cells adjacent to the node, the contact area, and the number of quadrature points (provided the corresponding integration is exact and the physical velocity field is the same). For a common positive factor \(\alpha\), \(\mathbf s_i(\alpha\mathbf W_i,\alpha D_i)=\mathbf s_i(\mathbf W_i,D_i)\); any partition into processes without overlap or omission satisfies \(\mathbf s_i=(\sum_r\mathbf W_{ir})/(\sum_r D_{ir})\), and the per-process ratios must not be averaged. This physical input should not depend on `normalize_c=true/false`. These are exact reproduction relations for constant fields, and do not claim that an arbitrary nonuniform solution is equal node by node on different meshes.

  **I2 — Secondary: isotropic velocity norm and the explicit time lag.** Define \(\delta=10^{-12}\) (the source's regularization constant in the current velocity units); within time step \(n\):
  \[
  \mu_i^n=F\left(0,\ p_i^{n-1},\ \sqrt{\|\mathbf s_i^{n-1}\|^2+\delta^2},\ 0\right).
  \]
  Here the four argument positions of the function are, in order, the time parameter and the spatial parameters `x`, `y`, `z`; that is, `x` is reused as the old contact pressure, `y` is reused as the old slip rate, and time and `z` are both zero. When there is no velocity history, set \(\mathbf s_i^{n-1}=0\), so the function receives \(\delta\), not an exact zero; the pressure still comes from `solutionOld()` and is not forced to zero. A retry after the same accepted state must not use the velocity history of the rejected trial. For any \(Q\in O(k)\), \(\mu(p,Q\mathbf s)=\mu(p,\mathbf s)\); reversing the sign of a 2D velocity or rotating a 3D tangent basis does not change the coefficient. Setting zero when there is no history is a discrete initialization convention, and cannot be interpreted as a body that has just entered the interface being necessarily at rest physically.

  **I3 — Secondary: Coulomb bound with the correct history coefficient and nonpositive friction power.** Restricted to currently persistent closure, real weighted gap equal to zero, \(p_i^n>\epsilon\), \(\mu_i^n\ge0\), \(c_t>0\), \(\Delta t>0\). On the converged solution we should have
  \[
  \|\boldsymbol\lambda_i^n\|\le\mu_i^n p_i^n;
  \quad \|\mathbf s_i^n\|>0\ \Longrightarrow\
  \boldsymbol\lambda_i^n=\mu_i^n p_i^n\frac{\mathbf s_i^n}{\|\mathbf s_i^n\|};
  \quad \mathcal P_i=-\boldsymbol\lambda_i^n\cdot\mathbf W_i^n
  =-D_i^n\mu_i^n p_i^n\|\mathbf s_i^n\|\le0.
  \]
  In sticking, \(\mathbf s_i=0\), and only the disk bound and zero slip power are required. This power is a nodal contribution in the sense of the discrete dual mortar pairing, and is not a pointwise assertion at an arbitrary interpolation point. For the normal-separation branch with \(p_i<\epsilon\), the source residual requires \(\boldsymbol\lambda_i=0\). Dynamic normal persistency allows a residual in the true gap, so in general dynamic states the radius \(\mu p\) cannot be used unconditionally: the actual radius in the source is \(\mu\max(0,p-c_{eff}\bar g)\); the physical-bound tests above deliberately choose \(\bar g=0\) or quantify its error.

  A constant `mu <= 0` should be rejected by parameter validation, which is an additional API contract of this PR; zero friction itself does not violate physics. The output of an arbitrary `function_friction` is not clamped or forced to be positive in this change, so an unconditional invariant that "all admissible functions are dissipative" cannot be proposed.

- Sources of the invariants (first principles / literature formula / consistency with other implementations in the library / dimensions):

  I1 comes from the reproduction of constants by the projection and the linearity of integration: \(\sum\psi wU=U\sum\psi w\). \(D\) is the contact measure and \(W\) is the contact measure times velocity, so only \(W/D\) has the scale of a physical velocity; dimensions alone cannot exclude the original unweighted-sum error, so constant reproduction must be checked. `timestepSetup()` in the merged [dynamic constraint implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C) and the [weighted normal constraint](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicWeightedGapLMMechanicalContact.C) give the integration, signs and history reading. The [quasi-static constraint](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C) also uses this ratio, but with the current state, so the two full transient solutions cannot directly be required to agree. The source cites Wohlmuth (2011), Eq. 3.10a; the original equation of the paper was not independently checked this time, the proof basis adopts the direct algebraic relations above, and the source's literature attribution is not treated as independent verification.

  The norm invariance of I2 comes from an isotropic scalar friction law; using the last accepted time step is the discrete contract of this public object, not a continuous physical law. I3 comes from the maximum-dissipation condition of Coulomb friction; the publicly exposed spherical-projection residual in [MortarContactUtils.h](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/include/utils/MortarContactUtils.h) gives an independently checkable algebraic statement, and [TangentialMortarMechanicalContact.C](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/TangentialMortarMechanicalContact.C) explicitly gives the sign convention in which the physical traction is opposite to the multiplier.

- Common operating domain (range of each input, with the basis):

  The mathematical validity domain is: 2D/3D, finite velocities and pressures, dual LM interpolation, positive and non-tiny \(D_i\), a non-degenerate mesh, positive time steps, and the contact and coefficient restrictions listed in I3. Use `use_displaced_mesh=true`, a planar fixed normal and a fixed tangent basis; do not add wear, temperature dependence, rotating surfaces, remeshing or restart at first. The source requires `newmark_beta > 0` and `newmark_gamma >= 0`; it is suggested to take the \(\beta=0.25,\gamma=0.5\) already in the tests, with consistent parameters for the dynamics and the constraints.

  Suggested random sampling domain (engineering test scale, not the full validity range declared by the authors): nondimensionalize with chosen units \(L_0,T_0,P_0\), in-plane length \(0.1\!\sim\!10L_0\), 1, 2, 4 cells in each direction with a non-matching discretization of the opposite face, \(p/P_0\in[0.1,10]\), \(\|\mathbf s\|/(L_0/T_0)\in[10^{-3},10^{-1}]\), plus exact zero velocity, all tangent angles and signs; \(\Delta t/T_0\in[10^{-3},2\times10^{-2}]\), keeping the displacement per step much smaller than the width of the contact support. Take \(\epsilon/P_0\le10^{-7}\), scale the positive \(c,c_t\) by the dimension of the residual, test both values of `normalize_c`; constant \(\mu\in[0.05,1]\). The default regularization is a hard-coded number, so when changing velocity units \(\delta\) must be interpreted accordingly; strict unit invariance at extremely small velocities is not claimed.

  The diagnostic function can be `0.2 + 0.02*x/P0 + 0.5*y/V0` (with actual numerical values substituted for `P0,V0`), which stays positive and monotone in velocity over the above domain. The existing test uses \(F(p,s)=0.3+0.5\,2.17^{-p/100}-10s\), which is a positive friction model only for \(0\le s<(0.3+0.5\,2.17^{-p/100})/10\); the velocity-weakening expression of that example cannot be extrapolated into a physical law at arbitrary velocity.

- Numerical check method (which public API of the merged commit, what to compute, what to compare against, suggested tolerance and why; if finite differences are needed, the step-size strategy):

  **Public entry points.** Later, on ContactApp of the merged version, configure through the `.i` input `[Constraints] type=ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact`, `function_friction`, `friction_lm`, the 3D `friction_lm_dir`, together with the public `NormalMortarMechanicalContact` / `TangentialMortarMechanicalContact`, the dual LM variables, `ParsedFunction`, and NewmarkBeta time integration. Use the existing `frictional-mortar-3d-dynamics-light-function.i` as the structural reference, making sure that the boundary/subdomain names of the UserObjects actually used match the constructed mesh. Export the normal and two tangential LMs through `NodalValueSampler`, output the Cartesian tangential multiplier vector through `MortarFrictionalPressureVectorAux`, and save every accepted step with Exodus/CSV. The in-class `oldNodalSlipRate()` and `computeFrictionValue()` are both protected, and calling them directly cannot be used as a public-API checking method.

  **Black-box observables for I1/I2.** Construct a small planar contact patch with sufficient overlap, apply normal loading to establish positive pressure, and keep the normal displacement as a solved degree of freedom; prescribe a uniform tangential motion of the contact surface by boundary conditions to guarantee persistent slip, avoiding using one top velocity in place of the unmeasured true interface velocity. Reconstruct the interface velocity of each accepted step from the integrator's actual velocity or its Newmark recurrence; for a uniform field the exact reference of the projection is that velocity itself, with no need to read the internal map. Using the positive monotone diagnostic function above, compute
  \[
  \mu_{obs,i}^n=\|\boldsymbol\lambda_i^n\|/p_i^n,
  \qquad \mu_{ref,i}^n=0.2+0.02p_i^{n-1}/P_0
       +0.5\sqrt{\|\mathbf U^{n-1}\|^2+\delta^2}/V_0.
  \]
  Compare the two only for nodes that are currently slipping and whose gap error is sufficiently small. To check nonuniform fields, independently reconstruct the dual test-function integrals \(W,D\) from the displacement, the actual velocity and the displaced mortar geometry of the same accepted step, and substitute their ratio into the reference function; another unverified "real tangential velocity" output must not be used as the ground truth. The monotone function can also be inverted, \(s_{reg,obs}=V_0(\mu_{obs}-0.2-0.02p^{n-1}/P_0)/0.5\), to compare directly with the reference norm.

  Change the mesh area, the number of adjacent cells, a sufficiently accurate quadrature order, `normalize_c` and 1/2/4 MPI processes, and compare \(\mu_{obs}\) for the same physical field; the pressure-dependent term must be deducted using the actual old pressure of each case. Change the velocity magnitude and pressure of two adjacent accepted steps to ensure that the old and new reference coefficients differ clearly, verifying that the old values are used; initialize from rest first, then do nonzero and varying slip. Test newly entering interface degrees of freedom separately for the convention "no history → zero-velocity norm regularization", distinguishing them from nodes that already had history. Use the `--test-restep` supported by the merged source to compare the history/LM output at the same accepted time points, checking that a retry does not contaminate the history; do not use the recovery feature, as the source explicitly does not support recover.

  **I3 and direction checks.** Use the above outputs to verify the disk bound, the sliding equality, and \(-\lambda\cdot W\le0\); in 3D use the Euclidean norm of the two tangential components, and one must not require each component separately to saturate. Reverse the velocity, rotate the velocity direction in the fixed plane, and rotate the whole coordinate setup, checking that the coefficient stays unchanged and the Cartesian multiplier transforms with the rotation. Also do paired computations with a constant `function_friction` and the same positive `mu`. The public C++ template `Moose::Mortar::Contact::frictionalContactResidual<Real,k>` can in a future small test be fed known \(\lambda,W,c_t,\Delta t,p,0,\mu,\epsilon\), verifying the same Coulomb root for both residuals `ONE` and `TWO`; this purely algebraic test cannot replace the normalization and history checks of the actual object. Test rejection of input with `mu=0` and negative values, and acceptance with a small positive value.

  **Tolerances.** For the algebraic check of the constant-field reference integral, a relative \(10^{-11}\) and an absolute \(10^{-12}V_0\) are suggested, for the summation error of a small double-precision mesh; for the post-solve \(\mu\) comparison start with a relative \(10^{-6}\) plus an absolute \(10^{-8}\), scale the traction error by \(P_0\), and scale the power by \(D_iP_0V_0\) to \(10^{-6}\). Require \(|c_{eff}\bar g|/p\ll10^{-6}\) and \(p\) far from the threshold; otherwise include the quantified gap/solver error in the tolerance, and do not misjudge it as an error in the velocity input. Tighten the nonlinear tolerance by an order of magnitude and check whether the error is stable; results from different processes allow floating-point reduction errors. No finite differences are needed; the Newmark velocity is not inferred from a first-order difference of adjacent displacement outputs, to avoid mixing time-discretization error into the velocity-normalization test. Comparisons across areas and meshes require only that the prescribed constant physical field agree with the same function input, not that the whole elastodynamic response be exactly the same.

- Formalization (Lean) feasibility: can the invariant be written as a proposition over finite-dimensional reals, and what are the main obstacles:

  **Yes.** With a fixed finite set of quadrature points, nodes and process partition, I1 is a proposition on real finite sums, nonzero division and linear maps, and the proofs of constant reproduction and partition aggregation are straightforward; the orthogonal-norm invariance of I2 also belongs to finite-dimensional real inner-product spaces, and the history convention can be expressed with a finite-state transition relation. I3 can, under the assumptions \(D,p,c_t,\Delta t>0\), \(\mu\ge0\) and zero gap, reduce by cases the disk projection or the two residuals being zero, and derive the sliding direction, magnitude and nonpositive power.

  The main obstacle is connecting the proof assumptions to the real implementation: dual mortar geometry and \(D_i>0\), consistency of the normal/tangential bases, the active set and edge dropping, owner communication without overlap or omission, the state advance of accepted/rejected steps, and floating-point error and the nonlinear-solver stopping conditions. It is not necessary to formalize the whole PDE first, but proving finite-sum equalities cannot prove these C++ lifecycle conditions; nor can the monotonicity of the total energy of the whole Newmark system be derived from the lagged friction dissipation.
