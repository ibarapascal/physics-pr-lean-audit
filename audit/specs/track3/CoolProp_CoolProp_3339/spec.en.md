## CoolProp/CoolProp#3339 fix(transport): rhosr-CS viscosity ignored x_crossover from the fluid file

- What changed (1–3 sentences, naming files and formulas):
  In `viscosity_rhosr` in `src/Backends/Helmholtz/TransportRoutines.cpp`, the liquid-branch weight is changed from `1/(1+exp(-100*(x-2)))` to `1/(1+exp(-100*(x-x_crossover)))`, reading the switching center already parsed from the JSON, and the fixed steepness 100 is named as a local constant. `src/Tests/CoolProp-Tests.cpp` adds a regression test that changes the switching center through the public JSON registration interface; the branch polynomials, the definition of the residual entropy, and the final viscosity scaling are all unchanged.

- Invariants (mathematical statements; write them as checkable equalities/inequalities, give variables, sign conventions, domain of validity):
  Unified notation: for a pure-fluid HEOS state the temperature is \(T>0\) [K], the molar density is \(\rho>0\) [mol/m³], the dimensionless residual Helmholtz energy is \(\alpha^r\), and \(\tau=T_r/T\). Use the signed molar residual entropy
  \[
  s^r=R(\tau\alpha^r_\tau-\alpha^r)\quad[\mathrm{J/(mol\,K)}],\qquad
  x=\rho s^r/q_c,
  \]
  where \(q_c\) is the JSON `rhosr_critical` [J/(m³ K)], which must be nonzero. Do not take an additional negative sign of the residual entropy; in the commonly dense states of the selected fluid, both \(s^r\) and \(q_c\) are negative and \(x\) is positive. Let \(a=\texttt{x_crossover}\) and \(k=100\), both corresponding to the dimensionless \(x\); \(L(x)=\sum_{j=0}^3 c_{L,j}x^j\), \(V(x)=\sum_{j=0}^3 c_{V,j}x^j\), \(C\) is the dimensionless scaling coefficient, and \(\eta_0(T)>0\) is the **dynamic viscosity** [Pa·s] of the same fluid other than the dilute-gas kinematic viscosity coefficient. Below, \(\eta\) always means dynamic viscosity.

  1. **Primary — logarithmic mixing in residual-entropy coordinates and positioning of the center.** For finite and valid model parameters and a single-phase state, define \(w=1/(1+e^{-k(x-a)})\); we should have
     \[
     \eta(T,\rho;a)=\eta_0(T)\{1+C[\exp(wL+(1-w)V)-1]\}.
     \]
     In particular, at \(a=x\) the normalized reference viscosity must be the geometric mean of the two branches:
     \[
     Z:=1+\frac{\eta/\eta_0-1}{C}=e^{(L+V)/2},\qquad C\ne0.
     \]
     This is not the geometric mean of the two end values of the final dynamic viscosity, because the affine scaling comes last. Far from the center, \(x-a\to+\infty\) selects the liquid branch and \(x-a\to-\infty\) selects the gas branch; the limits here are defined at a fixed state by moving the parameter \(a\), and do not require pushing the physical state to infinite density.

  2. **Secondary — no overshoot and conditional positive dissipation.** Let
     \(\eta_L=\eta_0[1+C(e^L-1)]\) and \(\eta_V=\eta_0[1+C(e^V-1)]\). For \(C>0\), we should have
     \[
     \min(\eta_L,\eta_V)\le\eta(a)\le\max(\eta_L,\eta_V).
     \]
     If both end values are positive, then \(\eta(a)>0\), consistent with the requirement that shear viscous dissipation be nonnegative; in particular for \(0<C\le1\) positivity holds automatically. Positivity cannot be asserted for arbitrary user JSON coefficients: for \(C>1\) one also needs \(\min(e^L,e^V)>1-1/C\). This check selects \(C=0.8089\) of R1233zd(E), so no additional assumption is needed.

  3. **Secondary — oriented, smooth response to the switching center.** Fix \(T,\rho\) and the other coefficients and vary only \(a\); then
     \[
     \frac{\partial\log Z}{\partial a}=-k\,w(1-w)(L-V),\qquad
     \left.\frac{\partial\log Z}{\partial a}\right|_{a=x}=-25(L-V).
     \]
     Therefore if \(L>V\), increasing the center must reduce the viscosity; if \(L<V\), the direction is opposite; if \(L=V\), changing the center cannot change the viscosity. This is a partial derivative with respect to a parameter, not an assertion of monotonicity in temperature or density along a state path. It must not be assumed that the polynomial labeled "liquid" is larger for all \(x\).

- Sources of the invariants (first principles / literature formula / consistency with other implementations in the library / dimensions):
  The residual-entropy relation comes from the Helmholtz thermodynamic identity \(s=-(\partial a/\partial T)_\rho\); \(\rho s^r\) has the same dimension as \(q_c\), so \(x-a\) in the exponent is dimensionless. The link between positivity and shear dissipation comes from \(2\eta D':D'\ge0\) for a Newtonian fluid. The specific mixing model is an empirical correlation and is not uniquely determined by thermodynamics: the center, bounds and derivatives above are mathematical consequences that the correlation must satisfy, and they alone cannot prove experimental accuracy.

  The merged version's [TransportRoutines.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/5418cec58c52183e3a010b2894e08b818ed4cc81/src/Backends/Helmholtz/TransportRoutines.cpp) gives the mixing, the final scaling and the dilute-gas formula; `parse_rhosr_viscosity` in [FluidLibrary.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/5418cec58c52183e3a010b2894e08b818ed4cc81/src/Backends/Helmholtz/Fluids/FluidLibrary.h) confirms that all parameters come from the JSON. The literature key of the [R1233zd(E) data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/5418cec58c52183e3a010b2894e08b818ed4cc81/dev/fluids/R1233zd%28E%29.json) is `Bell-PURDUE-2016-ETA`, and the [in-library bibliography](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/5418cec58c52183e3a010b2894e08b818ed4cc81/CoolPropBibTeXLibrary.bib) lists it as Bell and Laesecke, 2016, *Viscosity of refrigerants and other working fluids from residual entropy scaling*. This review did not read the body of the paper, so the physical validity of the fixed constant 100, or of an arbitrarily changeable center, is not claimed to have been verified by the original paper.

- Numerical check method (which public API of the merged commit, what to compute, what to compare against, suggested tolerance and why; if finite differences are needed, the step-size strategy):
  It is suggested that later work use the C++ public API; the declarations can be checked in [include/CoolProp/AbstractState.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CoolProp/CoolProp/5418cec58c52183e3a010b2894e08b818ed4cc81/include/CoolProp/AbstractState.h). Only a scheme is given this time; nothing was run.

  1. Obtain the single-element array with `get_fluid_param_string("R1233zd(E)", "JSON")`. As in the PR test, change `INFO.NAME` and `INFO.CAS` to unique identifiers, clear `ALIASES`, set `REFPROP_NAME="N/A"`, and remove the identity fields listed by the test; register with `add_fluids_as_JSON("HEOS", json_text)`. Use a new clone for each center; an existing fluid must not be overwritten and an old `AbstractState` object must not be reused.
  2. Build the state through `AbstractState::factory("HEOS", name)` and `update(DmolarT_INPUTS,rho,T)`, and screen with `phase()` and `p()`. Reconstruct \(s^r\) and \(x\) from `gas_constant()`, `tau()`, `dalphar_dTau()`, `alphar()`, and cross-check with `smolar_residual()`. Obtain \(\eta\) with `viscosity()`; the high-level `PropsSI("V","T",T,"Dmolar",rho,name)` should give the same quantity and unit.
  3. To avoid calling the internal dilute-gas function, register an extra **C=0 control clone**, with the other property parameters kept consistent, and call `viscosity()` at the same state to obtain \(\eta_0\). This is an isolating control implementable with the public API, not a physical calibration; use it only in the domain where the branch exponents are finite, to avoid 0 times infinity. It isolates the mixing behavior of this PR and does not independently verify the existing dilute-gas model.
  4. Independently compute the polynomials and the logistic (a stable piecewise implementation may be used), compute the full \(\eta_{pred}\) of invariant 1, and compare with the \(\eta_{API}\) of each clone: \(|\eta_{API}-\eta_{pred}|\le10^{-10}\max(|\eta_{pred}|,\eta_0)\). Also check the center equality and the branch bounds with \(y=\log Z\), with an absolute error bound of \(10^{-10}\max(1,|L|,|V|)\). These are double-precision formula-consistency tolerances, not experimental errors; they are far smaller than a percentage but allow for differences in EOS evaluation, JSON round trips and exponential rounding. For \(d=\pm0.4\), a more rigorous tail bound is \(|y-L|\le|L-V|e^{-40}\) or \(|y-V|\le|L-V|e^{-40}\), plus the numerical errors above; strict attainment of the limit in floating point is not required.
  5. When checking invariant 2, add an absolute tolerance of the same scale to the end-point bounds, and require positivity separately as \(\eta>0\) and finite. Positivity after log normalization must not substitute for positivity of the final dynamic viscosity.
  6. For invariant 3, register a clone at each of \(a\pm h\) at the fixed state, and compute \([y(a+h)-y(a-h)]/(2h)\). Near the center start from \(h=10^{-4}\) and halve successively to about \(10^{-6}\), looking for second-order error decrease and a stable plateau; a suggested derivative error bound is \(10^{-5}\max(1,|\partial_a y|)\). The step size is based on the switching width \(1/k=0.01\): too large averages the transition, too small amplifies rounding; in the saturated tails relative derivative error is not used. When \(|L-V|\) is too small, do only an absolute-error check, and do not decide the monotonic direction from noise.

  No exact dilute limit \(\eta\to\eta_0\) at an arbitrary center is additionally imposed: for this set of coefficients \(L(0)\ne0\), and at finite \(a\) the logistic liquid weight is strictly nonzero; the deviation at the default \(a=2\) is tiny, but moving to near zero makes this deviation significant. This is a limitation on the physical applicability of the empirical model parameters, and is not equivalent to the center-reading fix of this change being wrong.

- Formalization (Lean) feasibility: can the invariant be written as a proposition over finite-dimensional reals, and what are the main obstacles:
  **Yes.** Taking \(x,a,C,\eta_0\) and the two groups of four-dimensional coefficients as real parameters, all three propositions need only polynomials, exponentials, logarithms, order and single-variable differentiation; first prove \(0<w<1\), from which the geometric center, the absence of overshoot, and positivity under the end-point assumptions follow. The derivative proposition first requires proving \(Z>0\), and then uses the chain rule.

  The main obstacles are connecting the EOS residual-entropy output to this real-number model, proving that the specified state lies in the valid single-phase domain, and verifying the correspondence between C++ floating point, exponential overflow, JSON parsing and API dispatch on the one hand and the real-number semantics on the other. The EOS output can be taken as a hypothesis input in order to prove the mixing layer first; this cannot replace proofs of experimental accuracy, of the correctness of the EOS over its whole domain, or of the safety of arbitrary custom JSON.
