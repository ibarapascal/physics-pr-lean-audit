## CliMA/Oceananigans.jl#6083 Include drift velocities in the implicit part of adaptive implicit tracer advection

- What changed (1–3 sentences, naming files and formulas):
  `hydrostatic_free_surface_{ab2,rk}_step.jl` and `hydrostatic_free_surface_tendency_kernel_functions.jl` now jointly assemble the total tracer velocity $\boldsymbol U_c=\boldsymbol U_{transport}+\boldsymbol U_{bgc,c}+\boldsymbol U_{closure,c}+\boldsymbol U_{forcing,c}$, so that the adaptive explicit flux and the implicit solve use the same velocity. `advective_skew_diffusion.jl` is changed to compute the GM truncation with the full two-dimensional slope at each streamfunction staggered location, and to apply `minimum_bz`; `isopycnal_skew_symmetric_diffusivity.jl` converts the truncation parameters to the grid floating-point type. `derivative_operators.jl` and `mutable_immersed_grid.jl` add coordinate slope operators for the two staggered locations.

- Invariants (mathematical statements; write them as checkable equalities/inequalities, give variables, sign conventions, domain of validity):

  1. **Primary — the total drift velocity must not be lost in the explicit/implicit split, and the result must not depend on how the same velocity is decomposed by source.** Take $z$ positive upward, the concentration $c$, with no reactions and no physical diffusion. On each vertical face and for the actual substep length $\Delta\tau>0$, let $w=w_{transport}+w_{bgc}+w_{closure}+w_{forcing}$, $\alpha=|w|\Delta\tau/\Delta z$, and $s=1$ ($\alpha=0$) or $s=\min(1,C_*/\alpha)$. We must have
     \[
     w^e=sw,\qquad w^i=(1-s)w,\qquad w^e+w^i=w,\qquad |w^e|\Delta\tau/\Delta z\le C_*.
     \]
     A more direct public-API invariant is: in two models with the same grid, initial values, time step, boundaries and total velocity at every stage, $c_A^n=c_B^n$; that is, moving a fixed drift from the biogeochemistry interface to `AdvectiveForcing`, or splitting it between the two, should not change the concentration arrays. In particular, when two opposite velocities cancel, the total velocity is zero and $c^{n+1}=c^n$. The continuity equation $\partial_t c+\partial_z(wc)=0$ gives, for a constant $w$ and a pulse away from the boundaries, $\bar z(t)-\bar z(0)=wt$, with $\bar z=\int zc\,dz/\int c\,dz$; this is the physical benchmark that the numerical solution should approach, **not an identity required to hold bit for bit on a finite grid**. Concentration shapes for different $C_*$ may differ because of implicit upwind diffusion.

  2. **Secondary — conservation of the total tracer in a closed domain.** For a static grid, fixed volumes $V_i>0$, no reaction/source/sink/diffusive boundary flux, and zero total normal velocity at the boundaries,
     \[
     M^n=\sum_i V_i c_i^n,\qquad M^{n+1}=M^n.
     \]
     Pairwise cancellation of periodic face fluxes also applies; a one-dimensional nonuniform column uses $V_k=A\Delta z_k$, and the unweighted `sum(c)` must not be used directly. This condition does not require the drift velocity to be divergence-free. It can check whether the newly added implicit drift wrongly adds or removes mass, but passing it alone cannot prove that the transport velocity is correct; full-discretization positivity of WENO or conservation of tracer variance is not imposed as a guarantee of this PR.

  3. **Secondary — the GM slope truncation has full horizontal-norm dependence and produces a divergence-free eddy velocity.** Restricted to a static Cartesian grid, a single GM closure, and `κ_symmetric=nothing`. Take the buoyancy $b$ in m s⁻², stable stratification $b_z>0$, $m=\texttt{minimum_bz}\ge0$, $q=\max(b_z,m)$. For $q>0$, $\boldsymbol S=-(b_x,b_y)/q$, $S_m>0$, $\epsilon=\min(1,S_m^2/|\boldsymbol S|^2)$, with $\epsilon=1$ defined at zero slope, and $\boldsymbol P=\kappa\epsilon\boldsymbol S$. We should have
     \[
     0\le\epsilon\le1,\quad |\epsilon\boldsymbol S|\le S_m,\quad
     \boldsymbol P(R\boldsymbol S)=R\boldsymbol P(\boldsymbol S),\quad
     (u_e,v_e,w_e)=(-\partial_zP_x,-\partial_zP_y,\partial_xP_x+\partial_yP_y),\quad \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_e=0.
     \]
     $R$ is a horizontal orthogonal rotation; on the discrete grid only the corresponding analytic manufactured solution/grid-symmetry transformation is required to satisfy the rotation relation, and sampled fields under an arbitrary rotation are not required to be bitwise identical. $\kappa$ is in m² s⁻¹, $P$ in m² s⁻¹, and the eddy velocity in m s⁻¹. For $m=0,b_z\le0$, we expect $\boldsymbol P=0$ and all velocities finite; a positive $m$ artificially raises the denominator, so it can no longer be required that unstable stratification is switched off automatically. The Cartesian staggered differences should satisfy $D_xu_e+D_yv_e+D_zw_e=0$ up to rounding error. This discrete identity is not extended directly to active-topography grids here; that part needs a separate derivation of the volume/area metrics and the boundary conditions.

- Sources of the invariants (first principles / literature formula / consistency with other implementations in the library / dimensions):
  Invariant 1 comes from the conservative transport equation and the additivity of velocities; the $\bar z$ relation comes from multiplying the transport equation by $z$ and integrating, with the boundary terms required to vanish. The exact definition of the splitting parameter is in the merged version [adaptive_implicit_vertical_advection.jl](https://github.com/CliMA/Oceananigans.jl/blob/1734ff9e4509198f945c5e7740a0bccacc69a720/src/Advection/adaptive_implicit_vertical_advection.jl). Invariant 2 comes from the telescoping sum of finite-volume shared-face fluxes, and from conservation of physical particle number/tracer mass.
  In invariant 3 the slope comes from the isobuoyancy surface $db=0$, and the divergence-free relation comes from the commutation of mixed partial derivatives; the truncation formula is a model convention of the library, not a universal physical law. [ISSD constructor and truncation formula]$https://github.com/CliMA/Oceananigans.jl/blob/1734ff9e4509198f945c5e7740a0bccacc69a720/src/TurbulenceClosures/turbulence_closure_implementations/isopycnal_skew_symmetric_diffusivity.jl) cites Gent–McWilliams (1990), Redi (1982) and Gerdes, Koberle & Willebrand (1991); this specification is based on the library formulas actually read, and does not claim that the full text of the papers was independently checked. The sign and staggered locations of the eddy velocity are in [advective_skew_diffusion.jl](https://github.com/CliMA/Oceananigans.jl/blob/1734ff9e4509198f945c5e7740a0bccacc69a720/src/TurbulenceClosures/turbulence_closure_implementations/advective_skew_diffusion.jl). The docstring of the [rotation tensor source file](https://github.com/CliMA/Oceananigans.jl/blob/1734ff9e4509198f945c5e7740a0bccacc69a720/src/TurbulenceClosures/isopycnal_rotation_tensor_components.jl) writes stable stratification as (b_z<0$, which is inconsistent with the implementation's positive-denominator branch; this specification explicitly adopts the stable buoyancy gradient $b_z>0$ in upward coordinates, and does not copy that docstring's sign.

- Numerical check method (which public API of the merged commit, what to compute, what to compare against, suggested tolerance and why; if finite differences are needed, the step-size strategy):
  **1. Public construction and the total-velocity check.** Use `RectilinearGrid`, `ZFaceField`, `set!`, `HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel`, set `momentum_advection=nothing, free_surface=nothing`, and use `timestepper=:QuasiAdamsBashforth2` and `:SplitRungeKutta3` respectively. The tracer scheme is `WENO$order=5, time_discretization=AdaptiveVerticallyImplicitDiscretization(cfl=0.5))`. Implement the reaction-free drift through the extensions `required_biogeochemical_tracers` and `biogeochemical_drift_velocity(::..., ::Val{:D})` of `Oceananigans.Biogeochemistry.AbstractBiogeochemistry`, following the PR's `SinkingParticles`; the GPU version also needs `Adapt.adapt_structure`. `w` is zero at the two end faces and a given constant in the interior. Another model applies exactly the same face velocity through the public `forcing=(D=AdvectiveForcing(w=w),)`; a third splits the velocity between the two interfaces as $\lambda$ and $1-\lambda$, with $\lambda=0.25,0.5,1.5$, and finally an additional test uses exactly opposite velocities at the two interfaces. Each model should get an independent mutable advection object and initial state, and must not share time-step caches.
  Evolve through `time_step!`, read the result with `Array$interior(model.tracers.D))`, and compare $\|c_A-c_B\|_\infty/c_{scale}\le10^{-11}$ (Float64, within ten steps), with an initial Float32 threshold of $2\times10^{-5}$. Exact cancellation is also compared with the initial value. Read `znodes$grid, Center())` to compute the centroid: the PR baseline should descend by about 120 m, first with a 5% error limit; after extending the domain and halving $\Delta z,\Delta t$ simultaneously (fixed Courant number and physical end time) the error should decrease, and the small-time-step explicit result is then used as an independent convergence reference. It must not be required that the full concentration shape at a high Courant number equal the explicit solution, nor may boundary retention be misjudged as velocity loss. This directly constructible comparison covers the biogeochemical drift and the advective forcing; for the closure channel, a frozen-field replay comparison should also be made with the velocity generated by a single GM closure, requiring array equality only when the velocities at all stages are identical, and not making a strict equivalence comparison between two different flow fields that evolve with buoyancy.
  **2. Mass.** Compute $M$ at each of the above steps, including low/high Courant numbers, both time integrators, sign-changing spatial velocities, and nonuniform columns. A uniform column can reuse `sum$D)`; for a nonuniform column compute $\Delta z_k$ from the face coordinates given when the grid was defined. Require $|M^n-M^0|/\sum V_i|c_i^0|\le10^{-12}$ (three-step Float64 baseline), and $10^{-11}$ for the ten-step extension; Float32 uses $2\times10^{-5}$. These are starting tolerances of the order of a hundred to a thousand roundings, and the reduction method, scale and precision need to be recorded; a percentage tolerance must not be used to mask a conservation error.
  **3. GM manufactured solution and divergence-free check.** Use `HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModel$grid; buoyancy=BuoyancyTracer(), tracers=(:b,:c), closure=IsopycnalSkewSymmetricDiffusivity(κ_skew=κfun, κ_symmetric=nothing, skew_flux_formulation=AdvectiveFormulation(), isopycnal_tensor=SmallSlopeIsopycnalTensor(minimum_bz=m), slope_limiter=FluxTapering(S_m)))` (the closure types can be imported explicitly from `Oceananigans.TurbulenceClosures`). Let $b=G_xx+G_yy+Bz$, $\kappa$z)=\kappa_0+a(z+H/2)\ge0$, where $\kappa_0>|a|H/2$. `set!(model, b=...)` updates the closure fields, after which the diagnostic velocities are read with `interior(model.closure_fields.u/v/w)`, with no need to call the private slope/kernel functions; this update path is confirmed by the merged version's [set! implementation](https://github.com/CliMA/Oceananigans.jl/blob/1734ff9e4509198f945c5e7740a0bccacc69a720/src/Models/HydrostaticFreeSurfaceModels/set_hydrostatic_free_surface_model.jl).
  In the interior at least three cells away from all boundaries, independently compute $q=\max$B,m),S_x=-G_x/q,S_y=-G_y/q$, and expect $u_e=-a\epsilon S_x,v_e=-a\epsilon S_y,w_e=0$. A nonzero $a$ must be taken: a constant coefficient and constant slope give zero velocity in the interior and cannot distinguish an erroneous truncation. In particular for $S_x=S_y=S_m$, $\epsilon=1/2$, which can detect the old per-component truncation; swap x/y and rotate the gradient to re-check the norm dependence. The branches with zero slope, $\kappa=0$, and $m=0,B\le0$ must output finite zero velocities. After changing the linear buoyancy to a smoothly spatially varying field, take adjacent-face differences of the actual staggered velocity arrays and check $D_xu+D_yv+D_zw$, covering at least the cells whose full stencil lies in the interior. Check the manufactured velocities with an error limit of $10^{-10}U_{scale}$ (Float64) or $10^{-4}U_{scale}$ (Float32); the divergence tolerance takes the same relative coefficient times $U_{scale}$1/\Delta x+1/\Delta y+1/\Delta z)$. $U_{scale}$ is the velocity scale of the nonzero manufactured solutions in this batch, and a zero solution also uses this scale as its absolute tolerance. For a Float32 grid, keep the default Float64 closure parameters and test once more, checking that all outputs are finite and the precision relative to the Float64 baseline; CUDA is an optional compatibility re-check.
  The linear manufactured solutions above need no numerical differentiation of continuous functions; the divergence-free check uses the grid differences of the finite-volume definition itself. If extended to nonlinear manufactured solutions, use analytic derivatives as the benchmark and halve the grid size to check the truncation error; if the tendency is diagnosed with $$c(\delta t)-c(0))/\delta t$, halve $\delta t$ successively and look for the plateau after the error has decreased and before rounding dominates, rather than using a single extremely small step.

- Formalization (Lean) feasibility: can the invariant be written as a proposition over finite-dimensional reals, and what are the main obstacles:
  **Yes.** The velocity splitting is a real-number proposition with absolute values, min/max and a zero-velocity branch under $\Delta z,\Delta\tau,C_*>0$; mass conservation can be expressed as a left invariant between the volume vector and the explicit flux difference and the action of the implicit matrix, and after proving the cancellation of boundary faces and the invertibility of the implicit matrix it can be composed up to the AB2/RK substeps. The GM truncation bound and orthogonal covariance are two-dimensional algebra, and the vanishing divergence on the staggered grid is a finite-dimensional identity caused by the commutation of finite-difference matrices.
  The main obstacle is connecting these mathematical objects to Julia's actual dispatch, staggered locations, halo/boundaries and stage states; to prove full array equivalence one must also prove that the various sources assemble the same total velocity at every stage. Floating-point type conversion, division-by-zero intermediate expressions and the NaN selection semantics require a separate IEEE floating-point layer; connecting the continuous exact translation of the centroid to a discrete error bound requires convergence/boundary-tail analysis, and cannot be derived from the finite-dimensional velocity identities alone. Active grids also need a separate proof of the metric identities, which the Cartesian proof of this specification does not cover.
