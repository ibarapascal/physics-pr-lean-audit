## 1. What changed

PR #34020, **“Pass the nodal slip rate to dynamic mortar function_friction,”** changes MOOSE's contact module between base `aec6606a7f7d68a1b29550bdfd480b229ce65b9e` and merge `f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1`. This specification is based on the supplied diff and commit-pinned source inspection; no simulation was run.

Let \(\psi_i\) be the lower-dimensional multiplier test function, \(a_q=J_qw_q\,\mathrm{coord}_q\) the mortar quadrature measure, and \(\mathbf t_{i\alpha}\) a nodal unit tangent. The relative velocity used for friction is \(\mathbf v=\dot{\mathbf u}_s-\dot{\mathbf u}_p\), secondary minus primary. Define, over all mortar segments contributing to degree of freedom \(i\),

\[
 W_i=\sum_q\psi_i(q)a_q,\qquad
 V_{i\alpha}=\sum_q\psi_i(q)a_q\,
       \mathbf v(q)\cdot\mathbf t_{i\alpha},\qquad
 s_{i\alpha}=V_{i\alpha}/W_i.
\]

There is one tangential component in 2D and two in 3D. Superscript \(n\) below denotes an accepted time state; the subsequent solve uses its history.

* `modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C`: `computeQpProperties()` and `computeQpIProperties()` stop accumulating the separate, unweighted sum \(U_{i\alpha}=\sum_q\psi_i(q)\mathbf v(q)\cdot\mathbf t_{i\alpha}\). `timestepSetup()` now stores \(s_{i\alpha}^n=V_{i\alpha}^n/W_i^n\) on the owning process, instead of storing \(U_{i\alpha}^n\). It retains the existing early return on a retried step. `post()` and `incorrectEdgeDroppingPost()` retain communication of weighted velocities and remove communication of the obsolete unweighted map. `enforceConstraintOnDof()` and `enforceConstraintOnDof3d()` use the new non-inserting `oldNodalSlipRate()` lookup, which asserts local ownership and returns `(0,0)` if history is absent. `validParams()` now rejects constant `mu <= 0`.
* `modules/contact/include/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.h`: removes the unweighted current-velocity map and quadrature vector, documents the historical map as nodal slip rates, and declares the lookup helper.
* `modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicWeightedGapLMMechanicalContact.C`: `computeQpIProperties()` accumulates \(W_i\) unconditionally. Both post-processing paths pass `true` to the normalization-communication argument of `communicateGaps()`, so the owner receives the full denominator even when `normalize_c=false`. This does **not** change the user's `normalize_c` setting or automatically normalize the constraint's numerical factors.
* `modules/contact/doc/content/source/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.md`: documents the previous-step pressure and projected slip rate and cites Wohlmuth (2011).
* `modules/contact/test/tests/mortar_dynamics/gold/frictional-mortar-3d-dynamics-light-function_out.e`: replaces one binary Exodus reference. No test input, assertion, or test declaration is added by this PR.

The unchanged `computeFrictionValue()` now receives different inputs and evaluates

\[
 \mu_i^{n+1}=F\left(0,p_i^n,
       \sqrt{(s_{i1}^n)^2+(s_{i2}^n)^2+10^{-24}},0\right),
\]

where the arguments are function time, spatial `x`, `y`, `z`; here `x` encodes pressure and `y` speed, not physical position. In 2D \(s_{i2}=0\). Missing slip history therefore yields speed \(10^{-12}\), not exactly zero. Pressure still comes from `solutionOld()` and is not zeroed by the missing-history helper. The function receives time and temperature-coordinate zero. Constant friction continues to return `mu` directly.

## 2. Invariants

**I1 — Primary: consistent mortar projection and constant reproduction.** For each assembled, locally owned degree of freedom with finite \(W_i\ne0\), the history must satisfy \(W_i s_{i\alpha}=V_{i\alpha}\). If \(\mathbf v(q)\cdot\mathbf t_{i\alpha}=b_\alpha\) throughout that support, then \(s_{i\alpha}=b_\alpha\), independently of support measure, quadrature-point count, or segmentation, provided numerator and denominator integrate the same measure. Multiplying all measures on that support by any \(k>0\) leaves \(s_i\) unchanged. The result has velocity units; \(V_i\) alone has velocity times interface-measure units.

For a complete dual mortar basis satisfying \(\int\psi_i\phi_j\,d\Gamma=W_i\delta_{ij}\), the projection coefficients satisfy \(\int\psi_i(v_\alpha-\sum_j s_{j\alpha}\phi_j)\,d\Gamma=0\). This stronger projection interpretation requires that biorthogonality, appropriate integration, and support assumptions hold; the ratio and constant-reproduction identities are the direct code contract even on a truncated interface. It is the norm of a projected vector, **not** the projection of pointwise speed. Dual tests can change sign, so a convex-hull bound on the resulting velocity is not asserted.

**I2 — Primary: previous-accepted-step constitutive arguments.** During all nonlinear evaluations and retries of step \(n+1\), the function arguments must be \((0,p_i^n,\sqrt{\|s_i^n\|^2+10^{-24}},0)\), using the last accepted state's \(V_i^n\) and \(W_i^n\), rather than the current iterate or rejected attempt. For a degree of freedom with no stored slip history, \(s_i^n=0\). Domain: stable degree-of-freedom identity and ownership across the history transition, finite old pressure, and a valid interface weight for every stored velocity. Recovery is explicitly unsupported. Zero initialization for an entering node is a **model/implementation convention**, not evidence that the surfaces physically had zero relative velocity before entry; extrapolated history would be another possible model.

**I3 — parallel and numerical-option consistency.** For any partition of the same quadrature contributions into process-local sets \(Q_r\),

\[
 s_{i\alpha}=\frac{\sum_r\sum_{q\in Q_r}\psi_i a_qv_{i\alpha}}
                   {\sum_r\sum_{q\in Q_r}\psi_i a_q}.
\]

This must agree with serial assembly up to floating-point summation error. At a fixed accepted physical state, the constitutive arguments must be independent of `normalize_c` and `friction_projection_degree`. Both edge-dropping paths must compute this ratio for their retained degrees of freedom. Domain: identical geometry, quadrature, history, and active support; no claim of identical finite-tolerance trajectories, nor equality between edge-dropping algorithms when they retain different supports. The two normal-contact post paths already differ in their persistency treatment.

**I4 — tangential isotropy of a speed-only law.** For an orthogonal change of local tangential coordinates \(Q\), \(s'_i=Qs_i\) implies \(\sqrt{\|s'_i\|^2+10^{-24}}=\sqrt{\|s_i\|^2+10^{-24}}\) and hence unchanged \(\mu_i\) at the same old pressure. This includes sign reversal in 2D, either axis and mixed slip in 3D, and reduction of 3D to 2D when the second component is zero. Domain: an orthonormal tangent basis, identical physical support and pressure, an isotropic function depending only on supplied pressure and speed. This is not an assertion of general objectivity under time-dependent rotating frames.

**I5 — admissible Coulomb friction and dissipative sliding (conditional).** Constant-mode admissibility is precisely finite \(\mu>0\); zero and negative constants must be rejected by input validation. For a finite nonnegative function value, positive \(c_t\), positive time step and positive interface weight, define

\[
 C_n=\begin{cases}c/W_i&\text{if normalized}\\c&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \quad C_t=\begin{cases}c_t/W_i&\text{if normalized}\\c_t&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \quad R_i=\mu_i\max(0,p_i-C_ng_i),\quad
 q_i=\lambda_{ti}+C_t\Delta t\,V_i.
\]

Here \(g_i\) is the current weighted geometric gap, including any supplied wear contribution. At a zero tangential residual with \(p_i\ge\epsilon\), both projection degrees must satisfy

\[
 \lambda_{ti}=\operatorname{Proj}_{\overline B_{R_i}}(q_i),\qquad
 \|\lambda_{ti}\|\le R_i.
\]

If \(V_i\ne0\), \(\lambda_{ti}=R_iV_i/\|V_i\|\); if sticking, \(V_i=0\) and the multiplier lies inside the disk. The resisting traction on the secondary body has the opposite sign to this multiplier convention, so its discrete tangential power is \(-\lambda_{ti}\cdot V_i\le0\). For \(p_i<\epsilon\), the library deliberately uses the identity residual, giving \(\lambda_{ti}=0\). For closed contact with \(g_i=0\), the radius reduces to \(\mu_i p_i\). Use the augmented radius elsewhere: the dynamic normal constraint does not guarantee identically zero geometric gap.

This is conditional on \(F\ge0\) over its operating domain. The PR does not enforce this for `function_friction`. The supplied law \(F(p,s)=0.3+0.5\,2.17^{-p/100}-10s\) is nonnegative only for \(s\le[0.3+0.5\,2.17^{-p/100}]/10\). Normalizing speed cannot make an arbitrary law positive. Strictly positive constant friction is a library input convention; frictionless contact is physically meaningful but needs a different configuration. No exact conservation of total mechanical energy is asserted for this frictional, time-discretized problem.

## 3. Sources of the invariants

* **I1:** first-principles cancellation of a common integration measure and dual-basis projection. The PR cites Barbara Wohlmuth, *Variationally consistent discretization schemes and numerical algorithms for contact problems*, Acta Numerica 20 (2011), 569–734; its code comment identifies equation 3.10a. The bibliographic attribution is confirmed in the [contact bibliography](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/doc/content/bib/contact.bib); that equation was not independently inspected. The stated conditional projection identity is derived explicitly above. The [quasi-static constraint](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C) independently uses weighted velocity divided by normalization, although it evaluates current-state arguments and has other multiplier-scaling machinery; full dynamic/quasi-static solution equality is not implied.
* **I2:** the [merged dynamic friction implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicFrictionalForceLMMechanicalContact.C), its new documentation, and the retry/history lifecycle in the [dynamic weighted-gap implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/constraints/ComputeDynamicWeightedGapLMMechanicalContact.C). Previous-step evaluation and empty-history initialization are library conventions, not universal friction laws.
* **I3:** additivity of integration and the owner-reduction contracts of [communicateGaps](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/src/utils/MortarContactUtils.C) and `communicateVelocities` in [MortarContactUtils.h](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/f90f508580f7611f7d75ee458cd177affc7288d1/modules/contact/include/utils/MortarContactUtils.h).
* **I4:** Euclidean norm invariance under orthogonal transformations and the unchanged friction-function call.
* **I5:** Coulomb maximum-dissipation/contact complementarity and the explicit degree-one Alart–Curnier and degree-two Hueber–Stadler–Wohlmuth formulas in `MortarContactUtils.h`. The latter is \(\max(R,\|q\|)\lambda_t-Rq\), with an identity-residual fallback at \(R=\|q\|=0\); both degrees use the same pressure threshold. The dynamic documentation also cites Tal and Hager, *Dynamic mortar finite element method for modeling of shear rupture on frictional rough surfaces*, Computational Mechanics 61 (2018), 699–716, for stabilization context. No external standard supplies a mandatory slip-history initialization, speed regularization, or universal valid range for a user-defined friction function.

| Velocity components | Finite signed values. Test 0, \(10^{-12}\), \(10^{-8}\), \(10^{-4}\), 0.01, 0.02, 0.1 and 1 in length/time units, with both signs and mixed directions. ## 5. Numerical check plan

Use the merged Contact application through input files and its executable, e.g. `modules/contact/contact-opt -i patch.i`, with CLI overrides such as `Constraints/friction/normalize_c=true`. Start from the supplied `frictional-mortar-3d-dynamics-light-function.i` and the existing 2D `block-dynamics-friction.i`; build controlled planar patch variants with prescribed displacement histories and a normal load. Retain `use_displaced_mesh=true`, `interpolate_normals=false`, and compatible dual normal/tangential multiplier spaces. Configure matching interface names on the weighted-velocity user object and all force constraints in each new fixture.

**Observability through the public API.** `oldNodalSlipRate()` and `computeFrictionValue()` are protected, so do not propose calling them externally. Register a small test-app `Function` subclass as `function_friction`: override public `value(const ADReal &, const ADPoint &) const` and the Real overload to record the actual `(time,x,y,z)` arguments and return a specified smooth positive law. Preserve derivatives of that law in the AD overload. Log process, actual execution step and attempt separately: the passed function time is always zero. This uses the supported Function extension interface without modifying the constraint. Duplicate callbacks from residual/Jacobian evaluation are expected. For uniform patches compare every callback to the common oracle; for nonuniform cases compare argument multisets with independently computed nodal values, using distinct old pressures to disambiguate nodes where needed. Do not use callback order as node identity.

Output normal and tangential LMs with `NodalValueSampler`, and physical tangential components with `MortarFrictionalPressureVectorAux`, as in the supplied fixture. Output displacement/velocity fields and displaced coordinates for an independent quadrature calculation. Use an elemental sampler/Exodus elemental output for any valid elemental-LM case. The new recording Function is a proposed check fixture, not an existing MOOSE diagnostic.

* **I1:** prescribe spatially uniform relative velocity on a flat interface and inspect the second and subsequent steps. Compute the velocity from the actual discrete time integrator, not just the nominal boundary slope. Compare recorded speed with \(\sqrt{\|b\|^2+10^{-24}}\). Repeat with support lengths scaled by 0.1, 1 and 10, mesh refinements 1/2/4/8, nonmatching primary subdivisions, and adequate quadrature orders. Add an affine nonuniform slip field and independently integrate \(V_i/W_i\) using the dual tests and mortar segment measure; compare the norm after component projection. Include opposing slip within a support to distinguish “norm of projection” from “average speed.” Accept absolute speed error \(10^{-12}v_*\) plus relative error \(10^{-9}\) on prescribed patches, with \(v_*\) the chosen velocity unit; use \(10^{-7}v_*\) plus \(10^{-6}\) relative for solved, reconstructed fields. Tighten solver and quadrature settings to diagnose excess error rather than treating discretization error as a normalization failure.
* **I2:** impose an accelerating or piecewise-changing velocity and changing normal load for at least three accepted steps. Every callback in step \(n+1\) must match the output and independent projected velocity from step \(n\), even while current iterates change. Check the first step separately: speed \(10^{-12}\), old pressure from the initial solution, time and `z` exactly zero. Move the overlap boundary so a previously absent interface degree of freedom enters; expect the same zero-slip convention there. Run with `--test-restep` and compare callback arguments for corresponding attempts and final accepted solutions to the unretried run. Use the same speed tolerance as I1 and pressure tolerance \(10^{-9}p_*+10^{-7}|p|\). Compare within-attempt arguments exactly where they derive from identical stored doubles. Recovery is excluded, rather than silently simulated as an empty-history restart.
* **I3:** run the I1/I2 patches with `mpiexec -n 1`, `-n 2`, and `-n 4`, selecting partitions that split a secondary node's mortar support among processes. Compare owner callbacks by physical location/old pressure, not MPI rank or arbitrary IDs. Require speed agreement to \(10^{-10}v_*+10^{-8}|s|\). Cross `normalize_c=false/true`, `friction_projection_degree=ONE/TWO`, and `correct_edge_dropping=false/true`; set edge dropping consistently on normal and both tangential force constraints. Use prescribed kinematics to compare constitutive arguments without conflating different nonlinear trajectories. Include partially covered retained edge nodes and nodes excluded by the incorrect-edge path; excluded nodes should not produce enforcement callbacks. For constant `mu`, compare merge versus base fields in existing frictional and frictionless fixtures to \(10^{-5}\) absolute/\(5\times10^{-4}\) relative, the existing regression scale, to detect unintended consequences of always communicating weights.
* **I4:** in 2D run \(+b\) and \(-b\). In 3D run \((b,0),(0,b),(-b,0),(0,-b),(3b/5,4b/5)\) and their reversals; also rigidly rotate the planar geometry and imposed fields together. Match old pressure and projected speed. Expect equal callback speed and law value to \(10^{-10}\) relative plus the I1 absolute tolerance. A single scalar-law callback cannot reveal component signs; use physical force outputs and I5 to check the force direction.
* **I5:** use constant `mu=0.3` and a constant-valued `ParsedFunction` returning 0.3 in otherwise identical runs; their accepted solutions should agree to \(10^{-7}p_*+10^{-6}|p|\). Check input-only validation with `mu=0`, `mu=-0.1`, neither law, and both law types; require failures, and success for positive constants with no function. For positive smooth pressure/speed laws, sample separated, sticking, incipient-slip and clear-sliding states in both dimensions and both projection degrees. Evaluate \(R_i,q_i\) from output and independent quadrature; check the projection identity with tolerance \(10^{-7}p_*+10^{-6}\max(\|\lambda_t\|,R_i)\), the disk bound with the same tolerance, and \(\lambda_t\cdot V_i\ge-\tau_p\|V_i\|\). For strict sliding also check alignment and radius saturation. Probe pressures immediately below, at and above `epsilon`; if a whole-problem load cannot reliably resolve the threshold, call the public C++ `Moose::Mortar::Contact::frictionalContactResidual<Real,N>()` for `N=1,2` at those values, including \(R=\|q\|=0\). Those helper checks supplement, not replace, end-to-end verification of the corrected function arguments. A negative-valued function is an out-of-domain diagnostic: do not expect rejection or passivity from this PR.

The core prescribed-patch matrix covers all 2D/3D × normalization × projection-degree × edge-dropping combinations with a function law, including both 3D tangential components; constant-law comparisons cover both dimensions and projection degrees. Add zero and nonzero prescribed `wear_depth` with known gap contributions to ensure the weight remains geometric, independent of wear. Cover initial gap-detection and established-contact persistency states. Nodal dual LMs are the established configuration. The constructor also admits CONSTANT elemental friction variables: attempt a compatible dual elemental normal/tangential setup and check I1–I3 if supported. This branch is **uncertain** because map keys are chosen from multiplier nodality whereas communication's `_nodal` flag comes from the displacement family; a rejected or incompatible setup must be reported, not counted as coverage. Degenerate zero-weight supports, arbitrary ownership migration, higher-order nonconstant elemental friction variables, and recovery are outside the proved/checkable domain specified here.

## 7. Expected verdict

* **I1: expected pass** on nondegenerate compatible dual nodal interfaces. The numerator and denominator now use the identical test function, quadrature measure and old state. Zero or near-cancelled weights have no explicit guard; their behavior is outside this expectation.
* **I2: expected pass, with lifecycle uncertainty.** The lookup and retained retry guard implement the stated convention. End-to-end accepted-state retention, especially entry after partial overlap and rejected attempts, still needs execution. Ownership migration is not established by this pointer-keyed history design.
* **I3: expected pass for stable nodal ownership; uncertain for elemental configurations.** Both post paths now communicate the denominator even without numerical-factor normalization and history is formed only by owners. Matching MPI sums and safe map-key conventions require the proposed tests.
* **I4: expected pass** for the stated orthonormal-basis domain because the coefficient uses a Euclidean norm of the projected components. A global coordinate transformation test would also exercise tangent generation, which this PR does not change.
* **I5: expected pass conditionally** for positive constants and nonnegative finite functions at converged states. Rejection of zero/negative constants is explicit. An unconditional assertion that the new code always produces positive friction coefficients or dissipative contact would **fail**: user functions are unchecked and the supplied weakening expression becomes negative above its stated speed threshold.

These are source-based expectations, not recorded numerical outcomes.

## 9. Formalization feasibility

**I1, I3 and I4 are readily expressible over real arithmetic in Lean.** Represent quadrature supports by finite sets, weights and vector components by real-valued functions, and assume a nonzero denominator. Constant reproduction, cancellation under common scaling, partition-additivity, and orthogonal norm invariance are finite-sum and linear-algebra results. Add dual-basis biorthogonality as a hypothesis to derive the weak projection identity; proving that hypothesis for MOOSE's actual generated mortar geometry is a separate finite-element verification task.

**I2 is feasible as a small state-machine proof:** finite maps keyed by abstract degrees of freedom, an owner predicate, an accepted-step transition, a retry identity transition, and a default-zero lookup. The main obstacle is connecting that model to actual framework callback order, accepted versus rejected residual buffers, mesh changes, pointer lifetime, and MPI ownership, rather than the lookup algebra itself.

**I5 is feasible conditionally:** prove equivalence of the two residual zero sets with projection onto a closed Euclidean ball for nonnegative radius, treating the zero-radius/zero-norm and pressure-threshold branches separately. From positive \(C_t\Delta t\) derive the multiplier/slip alignment and discrete dissipation inequality. Strict constant-parameter validation is a simple predicate. Positivity of an arbitrary user `Function` cannot be proved without a supplied domain-specific hypothesis; for the test expression its admissible speed bound can be stated explicitly.

A proof over transcribed real-valued functions does not prove the C++ implementation, floating-point accuracy, AD derivative bookkeeping, MPI reduction, convergence of the nonlinear solve, or physical consistency of a chosen constitutive law. End-to-end verification would additionally require bounded rounding analysis near small weights and nonsmooth branches, a formal interface-geometry model, and a refinement argument connecting the framework's distributed history to the transcribed state machine.
