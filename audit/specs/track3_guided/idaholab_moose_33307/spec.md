## 1. What changed

This specification reviews idaholab/moose PR #33307, “Use log10 in relevant SCM closures and update transient regime calculation,” at merge commit `b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9`, relative to base `65e1f3e35d89ac753c08913532da02fdadc0a536`. Here “transition” denotes the laminar–turbulent regime, not time dependence. The supplied diff and commit-pinned source were inspected; no compiled-library checks were run.

Write (r=P/D), (H) for wire lead, (d_w) for wire diameter, (A_b,D_{h,b}) for assembly flow area and hydraulic diameter, and (R_i) for local Reynolds number. The common regime thresholds are
\[
R_L=320\,10^{r-1},\qquad R_T=10^4\,10^{0.7(r-1)},\qquad
\psi=\frac{\ln(R_b/R_L)}{\ln(R_T/R_L)}.
\]

* `modules/subchannel/src/problems/SubChannel1PhaseProblem.C` and corresponding `include/problems/SubChannel1PhaseProblem.h`: new `computeBulkReynoldsNumber()` and public `getBulkReynoldsNumber()`. With inlet channel mass flows (m_i) and viscosities (mu_i),
  \[
  M=\sum_i m_i,\quad \mu_b=\frac{\sum_i m_i\mu_i}{M},\quad R_b=\frac{M D_{h,b}}{\mu_b A_b}.
  \]
  `_bulk_Re` starts at 1; computation occurs on processor 0 after `initializeSolution()` and again for end-of-solve reporting. Previously the bulk value was computed only for reporting. In `implicitPetscSolve()`, the crossflow estimate now reads entry (g+N_g k), rather than the first adjacent channel index plus (N_c k). The estimate has gap, not channel, layout. The new mean absolute mass-flow calculation is diagnostic; resistance inflation still divides by minimum absolute mass flow.
* `modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMFrictionUpgradedChengTodreas.C` and `include/scmclosures/SCMFrictionUpgradedChengTodreas.h`: rename from `SCMFrictionUpdatedChengTodreas`, including object registration. `computeFrictionFactor()` dispatches to triangular or quadrilateral lattice functions. Both now use (R_e=\max(R_i,1)) in (f_L=C_L/R_e), (f_T=C_T R_e^{-0.18}), but use **inlet bulk** (R_b) for regime selection and (psi):
  \[
  f=\begin{cases}f_L&R_b<R_L,\\
  f_L(1-\psi)^{1/3}(1-\psi^7)+f_T\psi^{1/3}&R_L\le R_b\le R_T,\\
  f_T&R_b>R_T.\end{cases}
  \]
  Triangular wire sweep changes from natural log to (W_{s,T}=19-11\log_{10}(H/D)), (W_{s,L}=W_{s,T}). Edge/corner corrections multiply bare (C_T) by ([1+W_{s,T}(A_r/A'_p)\tan^2\theta]^{1.41}), and (C_L) by the bracket itself. Here (	an\theta=\pi(D+d_w)/H); the edge and corner projected/bare areas differ. Center wire drag remains based on ([19.56-98.71(d_w/D)+303.47(d_w/D)^2](H/D)^{-0.541}), with laminar multiplier 1.4. Polynomial coefficients were renamed, not turned into user parameters; their values are unchanged. Triangular coefficient-table selection uses (P/D) for center and (W/D=(D+\text{duct gap})/D) for edge/corner, each split at 1.1. Quadrilateral table selection uses (P/D<1.1) for all three groups, although edge/corner polynomials are evaluated at (W/D-1).
* `modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMMixingChengTodreas.C`: `computeMixingParameter(g,iz)` and `computeSweepFlowMixingParameter(g,iz)` switch regime selection and interpolation from local gap Reynolds number to (R_b). Local Reynolds still drives range warnings. For either coefficient (C=C_m) or (C_s), the transition law is (C_L+(C_T-C_L)\psi^{2/3}), with constant laminar/turbulent branches. Interior mixing is (\beta_m=C_m\sqrt{A_{r1}/A'_1}\tan\theta) if either adjacent channel is CENTER; otherwise zero. Sweep is (\beta_s=C_s\sqrt{A_{r2}/A'_2}\tan\theta) only if both channels are EDGE/CORNER; otherwise zero. The one-ring and multiple-ring coefficient branches remain distinct.
* `modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMHTCBorishanskii.C`, `computeNusseltNumber()`: changes the base term to (B(r)=24.15\log_{10}(-8.12+12.76r-3.65r^2)). Its turbulent candidate is
  \[
  N_B=B(r)+0.0174[1-e^{6-6r}]\,[\max(Pe_*-200,0)]^{0.9}.
  \]
  (Pe_*=Pr\,R_*), where the existing base helper uses (R_*=R_T) for (R_L<R_i<R_T), and (R_*=R_i) otherwise. The returned value is the existing HTC blend described below, not always (N_B).
* `modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMHTCGnielinski.C`, `computeNusseltNumber()`: for (a=f/8), (p=Pr+0.01), replaces denominator (d=1+12.7\sqrt a(p^{2/3}-1)) by (d_e=\max(d,0.1)), warning when (d<0.1). Its candidate is (N_G=a(R_i-1000)p/d_e). Unlike Borishanskii, it uses actual local (R_i), not the turbulent-endpoint helper.

The unchanged `SCMHTCClosureBase.C` returns (N_\ell=3.73,3.59,3.52) for CENTER, EDGE, CORNER when (R_i\le R_L), returns the candidate when (R_i\ge R_T), and otherwise returns ((1-w)N_\ell+wN_X), (w=(R_i-R_L)/(R_T-R_L)). `computeHTC()` returns (h=Nu\,k/D_h), (D_h=4S/\mathcal P), rejecting negative or nonfinite HTC.

`modules/subchannel/src/meshgenerators/SCMTriAssemblyMeshGenerator.C` adds (P>D) and fuzzy (d_w\le P-D) validation. Inputs adopt the renamed friction closure; sodium-19pin duct HTC switches to Dittus–Boelter. Regression CSVs are regenerated, some tolerances/configurations change, and the invalid corner-wire correction test is deleted. Documentation clarifies the Borishanskii logarithm and Gnielinski floor; Kim–Chung natural logarithms are explicitly retained. THORS/EBR-II plotting scripts and large-media changes affect presentation, not solver equations.

## 2. Invariants

**I1 — Primary: correlation and regime fidelity.** For admissible positive geometry and (M>0), the returned friction and mixing coefficients must equal the laws in section 1 with the same assembly (R_b) for every channel/gap. Local (R_i) still determines (f_L,f_T). In particular, holding geometry and (R_b) fixed while changing local flows must not change (psi,C_m,C_s). For (R_L<R_T), transition (psi\in[0,1]); (f(R_L)=f_L), (f(R_T)=f_T), and the friction law is continuous at these regime boundaries. Mixing satisfies (C_L\le C\le C_T), is nondecreasing in (R_b), and attains its endpoints. Friction interpolation is **not** a convex blend: do not assert that it lies between (f_L,f_T), or is globally monotone. No exact continuity at coefficient-table boundary 1.1 is claimed for rounded empirical fits.

**I2 — Primary: Borishanskii normalization and heat-transfer consistency.** For (1.1\le r\le1.5), (Pr>0), (R_i\ge R_T), and (0\le Pe\le2200), returned (Nu=N_B) with (Pe_*=Pe). For (Pe\le200), it is exactly (B(r)>0); above 200 it increases with (Pe) and is continuous at 200. At fixed (r,Pr), the library's HTC blend must attain its laminar and turbulent endpoints continuously and remain positive. For all accepted (S,\mathcal P,k>0), (hD_h/k=Nu). The positive low-(Pe) plateau is a turbulent-candidate property; the library returns (N_\ell) in its laminar branch even where a standalone Borishanskii formula would return (B(r)).

**I3 — Primary: finite, passive regularized Gnielinski heat transfer.** For finite (f\ge0), (Pr>0), (R_i\ge\max(R_T,1000)), finite positive geometry and conductivity, the merged prescription must give finite (Nu\ge0),
\[
0\le Nu\le10(f/8)(R_i-1000)(Pr+0.01),
\]
with equality to the unregularized formula when (d\ge0.1), continuity at (d=0.1), and the documented warning below it. This bound assumes inputs/products do not overflow machine arithmetic. The same passivity requirement applies to the returned transition blend, but needs separate verification for (R_L<R_i<1000): the candidate numerator is negative there and flooring the denominator is not a proof of nonnegative blended (Nu). A rejected negative HTC protects downstream temperatures but is not successful prediction in an advertised operating domain. The floor 0.1 and shift 0.01 are library modelling choices, not thermodynamic constants. They imply neither a constant (Nu) as Reynolds varies nor quantitatively correct liquid-metal heat transfer.

**I4 — Primary: crossflow incidence and mass conservation.** At each axial index (k), let (x_{g,k}) be the estimated gap crossflows and (B_{cg}\in\{-1,0,1\}) their oriented incidence matrix. The estimate must obey
\[
s_{c,k}=\sum_g B_{cg}x_{g,k},\qquad \sum_c s_{c,k}=0,
\]
with storage (x[g+N_gk]). Each internal gap has opposite signs in its two channels. This identity applies to arbitrary estimates, independent of convergence. For a closed-sided, steady, source-free assembly it entails \(\sum_c m_{c,\mathrm{out}}=\sum_c m_{c,\mathrm{in}}\) at a converged solution. Transient checks must include mass accumulation; inlet–outlet equality alone is not an invariant then. Relabeling gaps/channels consistently must not alter physical results. The changed code computes a relaxation estimate, not the final continuity matrix; final mass conservation alone is insufficient to validate its indexing.

**I5 — geometry admissibility.** Triangular mesh construction must reject (P\le D), reject (d_w>P-D+\epsilon_g), and accept exact wire-to-gap equality provided all other mesh constraints hold. Here (epsilon_g) is the actual default absolute tolerance of `MooseUtils::absoluteFuzzyGreaterThan` in this commit; this numerical convention must be read from the framework before testing. The mathematical noninterpenetration condition is (0\le d_w\le P-D); the fuzzy implementation admits a narrow tolerance band. No scale-independent relative tolerance is promised.

**I6 — zero-flow regularity and the limits of the new clamp.** At fixed finite (R_b>0) and admissible (C_L,C_T), local (R_i\downarrow0) must produce a finite friction coefficient, constant for (0\le R_i\le1), and vanishing friction force proportional to (f u|u|). This is a numerical regularization, not the exact creeping-flow (f\propto1/R_i) law below (R_i=1). Separately, for an all-inlet-flow shutdown (m_i=t\widehat m_i\), (\widehat m_i\ge0\), \(\sum\widehat m_i>0\), fixed positive viscosities, the physical limiting (R_b\to0) as (t\downarrow0). An implementation claiming zero-flow support should represent that limit at (t=0) without NaN or explicitly reject it. Mixed forward/reverse streams with (M=0) are outside this mass-weighted inlet convention.

The mixing documentation still writes unqualified (Re) in its interpolation equation; the merged code explicitly uses bulk Reynolds.
* I2 uses the [Borishanskii documentation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/doc/content/source/scmclosures/SCMHTCBorishanskii.md), citing `borishanskii1969heat`, and [HTC base implementation](https://github.com/idaholab/moose/blob/b5a1d9e3df14e98850e514f3e57d02c25c44fca9/modules/subchannel/src/scmclosures/SCMHTCClosureBase.C). Use SI units: lengths m, area m², viscosity Pa·s, mass flow kg/s, conductivity W/(m·K), pressure Pa and temperature K. Re, Pr, Pe and all closure coefficients are dimensionless.

## 5. Numerical check plan

Use a small compiled test application linked to the **merged** SubChannelApp, loading standard MOOSE input files with `[SCMClosures]` objects. Add an observing UserObject/postprocessor that calls the existing public C++ interfaces; this is necessary because no standalone Python closure API or scalar CLI evaluator is provided. Do not replace the closures with Python transcriptions. Use an independent double/high-precision oracle for comparison. A common scalar tolerance is
\( |y-y_{ref}|\le10^{-12}+10^{-10}|y_{ref}|\).
Use exact equality only for explicit zero branches and error text; permit small roundoff at branch endpoints. Run warnings as nonfatal while capturing them.

**I1.** Load triangular and quadrilateral problems and call `getFrictionClosure()->computeFrictionFactor(FrictionStruct(i_ch, Re, S, w_perim))`. Establish positive inlet states through input conditions and the normal solve lifecycle; read `getBulkReynoldsNumber()` afterward. Independently sum inlet (mu_i m_i) and compare (R_b). Repeat uniform/nonuniform viscosities and flow partitions at identical (M,mu_b), then transient inlet changes to detect stale bulk values. Do not evaluate closures before bulk initialization and call that a physical result.

For each lattice cover CENTER, EDGE, CORNER; triangular bare and wrapped; both sides and exact equality of every table switch at 1.1 (use offsets (10^{-6}\)); wire (H/D=8,20,52\), (d_w/(P-D)=0.1,0.5,1\). Cross (R_b=0.5R_L,R_L,\sqrt{R_LR_T},R_T,2R_T\) with local (R_i=0,0.1,1,50,10^3,10^5\), intentionally making local and bulk regimes disagree. Compare to the unchanged coefficient tables plus section 1's corrected wire factors. Check endpoint values directly; one-sided continuity probes must compare with the oracle rather than demand a linear-in-step tolerance, since cube-root endpoint slopes can diverge.

For mixing, obtain the configured `SCMMixingChengTodreas` UserObject and call **both** `computeMixingParameter(g,iz)` and `computeSweepFlowMixingParameter(g,iz)` for every actual gap at every axial cell. Cover center–center, center–edge and peripheral edge/corner combinations wherever present. Verify zero branches and endpoint bounds; vary local fields without changing inlet bulk state. Use ring counts 2,3,9 (7,19,217 pins), and one-ring separately as an extrapolation/code-coverage probe. Its interior branch may be unreachable in a valid one-pin mesh; record that fact rather than claim coverage. Compare (C_m) constants ((0.055,0.1)) versus ((0.077,0.14)), scaled by ((r-1)^{-1/2}), and (C_s) constants ((0.33,0.6)) versus ((0.413,0.75)), scaled by ((H/D)^{0.3}). Verify (R_b) and final outputs in serial and two MPI ranks; bulk computation is rank-0-only, so whether non-root access matters must be checked in the actual execution path.

**I2.** Call the configured Borishanskii object's `computeNusseltNumber(friction_args, NusseltStruct(Re,Pr,i_pin,iz,i_ch))` and `computeHTC(...,k)` for every channel type, valid pin indices, and both nominal and positive deformed pin diameter fields. Use (r=1.1,1.2,1.35,1.5\); fix (R_i>R_T\) and choose (Pr=Pe/R_i\) for (Pe=1,199.999,200,200.001,500,2200\). Compare absolute values, not just trends, to distinguish (log_{10}) from (ln\). Separately sweep local (R_i) through (R_L,R_T\) at fixed (Pr\), applying the base blend and endpoint (Pe_*\) correctly. Probe (Pe>2200\) only for documented warning/extrapolation, and assigning Borishanskii as duct closure for the unsupported-use error. Scale all lengths by 0.1 and 10 with dimensionless groups held fixed: (Nu,f,\beta\) must be unchanged and (h\) scale inversely with length at fixed (k\).

**I3.** Call Gnielinski through the same Nusselt/HTC public methods, for pins and the duct sentinel `std::numeric_limits<unsigned int>::max()`, all channel groups, triangular and quad meshes. Read the actual configured friction closure's (f\) for the oracle. Sweep (Pr=10^{-5},0.001,0.005,0.01,0.5,1,10,2000\), laminar/transition/turbulent local Re, and independently varied bulk regimes. For (p<1\), the floor boundary is (f_c=8[0.9/(12.7(1-p^{2/3}))]^2\). Tune admissible friction inputs around (f_c(1\pm10^{-6})\) and (f_c\); confirm the measured (f\) actually straddles the threshold. If it cannot be reached by a chosen geometry, select another configuration rather than claim branch coverage. Check formula, bound, warning and continuity at the floor; test both signs of the unfloored denominator. Densely sample (R_L<R_i<1000\) where nonempty to assess blended passivity independently. (Pr\) just outside the documented limits is a warning-path probe only. No comparison to regenerated gold can establish experimental accuracy of the floor.

**I4.** Through `subchannel-opt -i <case.i>` run asymmetric, unheated, closed-sided tri and quad assemblies with `implicit=true segregated=false`, `n_blocks=1,2,4`, and e.g. 16 axial cells. Excite multiple distinct crossflows using unequal inlet distributions or asymmetric resistance; include (N_g\ne N_c\) and later axial blocks, which expose the old stride error. Enable `full_output` and `verbose_subchannel`, inspect available mass-flow and summed-crossflow fields through public solution/postprocessor access, and check steady mass residual divided by (\max(|M|,10^{-12}\,\mathrm{kg/s})\le10^{-8}\) after tightening solver tolerances. Compare converged results with segregated implicit and explicit controls on the same discretization, relative tolerance (10^{-6}\) for nonzero flow/pressure differences. Repeat with consistent geometric symmetry/relabeling where the mesh API permits it.

There is an observability limit: local `sol_holder_P` and its signed per-channel sums are not exposed by a public getter. The log provides only a rounded maximum, after absolute values and a floor. Thus a public-API integration test cannot conclusively check arbitrary-vector incidence identity in this particular estimator. Treat mass conservation/convergence as partial evidence; a decisive unit test would require an additional observer or extracting that accumulation into a callable helper. For such a future test, use distinct signed values for every gap and axial plane and require cancellation to (100\epsilon_{machine}\sum_g|x_g|\). Do not claim that this stronger test is presently possible through an existing API.

**I5.** Execute mesh generation with (P/D=1,1-10^{-6},1+10^{-6},1.1\), and (d_w=0,P-D,(P-D)+0.5\epsilon_g,(P-D)+2\epsilon_g\). Read (epsilon_g\) from the pinned framework definition first. Ensure other geometry parameters are valid; assert pitch/wire-specific errors. Repeat a dimensionally rescaled case to characterize the absolute tolerance, not impose a relative one.

**I6.** Direct friction calls at local (R_i=0,10^{-12},0.1,1,1+10^{-6}\) with an initialized positive bulk flow check the finite plateau. Then run normal solver initialization with uniformly scaled inlet (t=1,10^{-3},10^{-6},0\), and inspect bulk Re and termination. Positive (t\) must give (R_b(t)=tR_b(1)\) for fixed properties; (t=0\) must either yield a finite defined state or an intentional diagnostic. Also try one zero-flow channel with (M>0\) in the implicit coupled solver: the still-present `_max_sumWij/min_mdot` division is a separate potential failure, not fixed by `Re_eff`. These are robustness probes outside the empirical fit range, not claims of validated natural-circulation/shutdown physics.

## 7. Expected verdict

* **I1: expected pass for positive initialized bulk flow and admissible coefficients; MPI/lifecycle coverage uncertain.** Source uses bulk Re consistently in both lattice friction branches and both mixing functions, preserving local Re powers. Warning fidelity is weaker: the triangular friction constructor reads the default bulk value 1 before the normal solve computes it, and its range checks disagree with its comments. These are not evidence that the positive-flow correlation itself is wrong.
* **I2: expected pass under the stated library blending convention.** The corrected logarithm and unchanged positive correction give the stated plateau and continuity. The result should not be judged against the unblended empirical formula in the library's laminar branch.
* **I3: expected pass for finite nonnegative friction and (R_i\ge\max(R_T,1000)\). Transition-region passivity is uncertain until sampled with coupled friction.** The floor removes denominator zeros but does not prove every blend positive, prevent overflow, or establish the documentation's claimed conduction-dominated physical accuracy. The latter remains unvalidated by this review.
* **I4: expected pass for the corrected gap-layout accumulation.** The new stride matches allocated vector dimensions and each internal gap should cancel. Full solver robustness and public-API observability remain separate limitations; no numerical run is claimed.
* **I5: expected pass within the fuzzy convention.** Exact equality should be accepted; invalid pitch is rejected before the wire comparison.
* **I6: local closure regularization expected to pass; whole-assembly zero-flow robustness expected to fail.** `computeBulkReynoldsNumber()` divides zero viscosity sum by zero total flow with no guard. NaN bulk Re then fails both ordered regime comparisons and reaches NaN interpolation. The new local Re clamp cannot repair this. A zero minimum channel mass flow also leaves the implicit resistance division unprotected.

## 9. Formalization feasibility

I1–I3 and the local part of I6 are suitable for Lean over real-valued transcriptions with explicit positive-domain hypotheses. Log-base conversion, positive denominators, endpoint identities, monotonic mixing and the Gnielinski bound are elementary real-analysis statements. Fractional powers require nonnegative bases; endpoint continuity uses continuity of real powers, not finite derivatives. Piecewise table coefficients can be represented as exact decimal rationals, while keeping empirical validity separate from mathematical correctness. No proof can derive experimental correlation accuracy or the numerical constant 0.1 from these formulas alone.

I4 is especially tractable: represent channels/gaps as finite types and the oriented incidence matrix as having one +1 and one −1 per internal gap. Prove sum cancellation and flattened-index bounds (g+N_gk<N_gN_z\) algebraically. Connecting that theorem to PETSc vectors, block offsets, MPI execution and the actual C++ loop requires a verified refinement or extraction step; proving a Python oracle is not proving the library implementation.

I5 can be formalized as inequalities plus the precise fuzzy-comparison definition. For I6, the positive-flow limit is provable, but the literal zero-flow implementation has an undefined real quotient and an IEEE NaN path; its total finite-output property is false without an additional guard/precondition. IEEE floating-point error, NaN propagation, `log10`/`pow` implementations, mesh-derived geometry, object initialization and nonlinear convergence are the principal obstacles to extending these real-arithmetic proofs to the complete compiled solver.
